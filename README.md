# The Unwritten Atlas

An original combination-discovery game with 144 elements and 178 recipes across four ages in a persistent historical Atlas. The simple two-slot workspace, inspectable Guide, local progress, and pointer, touch, and keyboard controls remain available across every unlocked age.

Origins is an explicitly fictional, undated prologue, not an archaeological period or a scientific account of creation. The historical pages draw on several traditions; their approximate dates are explained below, not a single worldwide timeline.

Desktop keeps Elements, Combine, and Guide visible together. Mobile uses a fixed three-tab navigator; choosing an element returns directly to Combine, while the Elements list marks occupied slots. Search matches names and categories, and the Guide separates collections from optional element research with native disclosure controls.

**Origins** is the fictional natural-world prologue. Discovering Life, Land, Tree, Rock, and Animal unlocks **Stone Age** (c. 3.3 million years ago–c. 3300 BCE), grants Human as that page's starting element, and keeps all earlier matter available. Stone Age landmarks are Stone Tool, Hearth, Art, and Village; optional discoveries remain open after its challenge is complete.

Origins has 41 elements, with optional waterways, aquatic life, and marine minerals: River, Lake, Algae, Fish, Salt, and Limestone deepen the natural world.

Stone Age spreads across hunting, cooking, shelter, art, fibres, clothing, pottery, farming, bread, and settlement. Stone Tool opens Field and Quarry without metal. Generic Metal Tool requires Stone Tool + Metal, so pre-metal human craft has its own foundation. Spear, Hearth, Shelter, Basket, Pottery, and Art now lead into Hunt, Home, Storage, Meal, and Cave Painting.

Stone Age has 42 elements. Bone, Needle, Leather, Tent, Flute, and Net broaden portable shelter, fine craft, and music. Wayfinding means reading landmarks and the sky, not using a magnetic navigation compass.

**Bronze Age** (c. 3300 BCE–c. 1200 BCE) unlocks after Village, Pottery, Metal, and Quarry, then grants Copper Ore. Its page branches through Copper and Tin into Bronze and Bronze Tool; through Metal Tool into Wheel and Cart; through Trade into Town and City; and through Papyrus into Writing and Scribe. Metalworking, records, and maritime trade extend through Forge, Anvil, Law, Harbor, and Voyage. Its seven landmarks are Bronze, Wheel, Writing, City, Forge, Law, and Voyage; completing its age challenge requires all seven and 23 Bronze Age discoveries.

Bronze Age has 41 elements. Irrigation, Canal, Granary, Loom, Textile, and Balance extend water management, shared harvests, weaving, and measured exchange. Polity covers political communities without implying modern nations; Writing Reed covers early reed brushes and clay styluses rather than feather quills.

**Iron Age** (from c. 1200 BCE, with regional end dates) spans twenty elements and nineteen recipes, including Lens as an optional later discovery rather than a Stone Age glass technology. Discovering Bronze, Forge, Law, and City unlocks it and grants Iron Ore. Ironworking, roads, markets, Steel, Coin, Aqueduct, Empire, and Manuscript are joined by Blacksmith, Nail, Saw, Timber, Bridge, and Wagon for specialized workshops and heavier construction. The age challenge still requires Iron, Iron Tool, Road, and Market plus six Iron Age discoveries, including the granted ore. All earlier matter remains available.

The expanded branches are optional: existing age unlocks, landmarks, discovery goals, and collection rewards are unchanged. Existing saves retain their earned progress; catalog completion percentages now include the additional discoveries. Classical Antiquity is reserved for a later update, not a fifth playable age yet. Age assignments organize gameplay rather than claim exact invention dates.

## Chronology and historical interpretation

The **Three-Age System** originated in the classification of Scandinavian archaeological collections by Christian Jurgensen Thomsen, published in 1836. It remains useful in particular European, Near Eastern, and other archaeological traditions, but is not a universal ladder of human development. Some regions have different sequences or no distinct Bronze Age. Stone, wood, bone, copper, bronze, and iron technologies also overlap rather than replace one another instantaneously.

| Page | Date label and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Origins | Fictional and undated. Its essences, life-making, and transformations are alchemical symbols, not historical events. |
| Stone Age | c. 3.3 million years ago–c. 3300 BCE. The opening anchor is the Lomekwi stone-tool evidence in Kenya, not the appearance of Homo sapiens. The closing anchor is a conventional Near Eastern Bronze Age transition, not the end of stone-tool use everywhere. This intentionally broad page combines Paleolithic and later Neolithic lifeways; it is not one regional chronology. |
| Bronze Age | c. 3300–1200 BCE is a conventional approximate Near Eastern/eastern Mediterranean frame, not a date for the first copper or the arrival of bronze in every region. Local chronologies differ: the National Museum of Denmark uses 1700–500 BCE for its Bronze Age. Earlier copper-working/Chalcolithic traditions are not a separate playable page here. |
| Iron Age | From c. 1200 BCE is an approximate Near Eastern transition toward wider iron use, not the first iron object. There is no global end date: the Ashmolean uses c. 800 BCE–43 CE for Britain. The former c. 500 CE endpoint has been removed because it mixed regional conventions into a misleading universal range. |

**Earliest evidence is not widespread adoption.** A rare artifact, experimental process, or first surviving written reference does not establish when a technology became common. Nor does absence of surviving evidence prove absence of a practice. Page placement may represent an intensified craft tradition or an optional later refinement, not invention. Dates and political labels are geographically dependent; Classical Antiquity can overlap iron-using societies rather than follow a worldwide Iron Age boundary.

### Unlock requirements are fictional gameplay gates

All four gates were reviewed and retained, with their historical limits made explicit:

| Page | Current gate | Historical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origins | Four starting essences; no unlock requirements | A fictional combination sandbox, not a reconstruction of geological or biological chronology. |
| Stone Age | Life, Land, Tree, Rock, Animal; Human grant | Natural-world discoveries organize the prologue. This is not a claim that humans appeared only after every listed discovery, or that all toolmakers were Homo sapiens. |
| Bronze Age | Village, Pottery, Metal, Quarry; Copper Ore grant | A settlement-and-materials gameplay milestone. Neither pottery nor a formal stone quarry is a universal prerequisite for metallurgy, and native copper and copper smelting predate widespread bronze. The grant does not date the discovery of copper ore. |
| Iron Age | Bronze, Forge, Law, City; Iron Ore grant | A workshop-and-civic gameplay milestone. Ironworking does not universally require a preceding Bronze Age, written law, or cities. These are not claims about how every society adopted iron. |

Landmarks, grants, discovery goals (18 / 16 / 23 / 6), and rewards are unchanged. Recipe inputs likewise express game associations, not necessary historical prerequisites or workable manufacturing instructions. Alternate routes do not mean societies had to follow the same path.

### Element review

All **144 current element descriptions** were reviewed, including the 24 additions beyond the earlier 120-element catalog; the requested 115-element count is no longer current. Generic natural phenomena and plausible nonexclusive craft descriptions are retained rather than assigned unsupported invention dates. The material corrections are:

| Element or group | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Compass | Reworked as **Wayfinding**, remaining in Stone Age. Magnetic maritime navigation belongs much later (documented in medieval China and introduced in Europe in the late twelfth century); simply moving a mariner's compass into Iron Age would not solve that error. Existing magnetic ingredient pairs are explicitly symbolic, and an additional nonmagnetic route is available. |
| Lens | Moved from Stone to Iron Age as an optional ancient optical craft, not spectacles or a telescope. The Neo-Assyrian object often called the Nimrud Lens is probably a crystal inlay; its optical use is disputed and is not proof of widespread ancient lens technology. Earlier map and voyage routes remain available without Lens. |
| Nation | Reworked as **Polity** and moved to Bronze Age, reflecting city-states and kingdoms rather than modern nation-states. Neither cities, written laws, nor mapped borders are universal prerequisites for political organization. |
| Metal, Copper, Metal Tool, Tin | Origins Metal is explicitly symbolic, not smelting before humanity. Native copper-working and later copper smelting precede widespread bronze use. Tin-bearing ore must be smelted; washing alone does not produce tin metal. |
| Quill | Reworked as **Writing Reed**: an early plant-based clay stylus or ink brush, not a feather quill. Cut-nib reed pens are a separate later development. A plant-based route is added; the old animal/scribe association remains an explicitly symbolic compatibility route. |
| Writing, Papyrus, Law | Writing includes Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphic traditions. A clay-and-reed route avoids making papyrus or ink necessary for all writing. Papyrus is made from strips of pith, not generic pulped fibres. Law can be customary and oral, not necessarily written. |
| Road, Market, Trade | Retained on their gameplay pages with earlier paths, timber trackways, roads, exchange networks, and markets acknowledged. Exchange need not involve coins, carts, or ironworking; not every exchange system is a formal marketplace. |
| Plow | Reworked as **Ard**, a simple scratch plow often made of wood. Earlier digging sticks, hoes, and ards do not require bronze. It makes shallow furrows, not the soil-turning action of a later moldboard plow. |
| Glass | Bronze Age placement is retained for early beads and vessels, often colored or opaque. Silica needs flux and controlled heat. Later glassblowing (first century BCE), clear optical glass, window glass, and spectacles are not implied by the early material. |
| Brick, Lodestone, Human, Primordial Soup, Dough, Cave Painting | Brick becomes **Mud Brick**, distinct from widespread fired brick. Magnet becomes naturally occurring **Lodestone**, not manufactured prehistoric magnets. Human represents hominin makers; life's origins and cave art meanings are not presented as settled facts. Early dough need not be leavened. |
| Boat, Anchor, Bellows, Iron, Bloomery, Sickle, Steel | Earlier watercraft, stone anchors, bellows, and non-iron sickles are acknowledged. Rare and meteoritic iron precedes wider ironworking. Bloomery reduction produces a solid bloom, not cast liquid iron; steel properties depend on composition and heat treatment. |
| Empire, Manuscript, Irrigation, Granary, Loom, Textile | Earlier empires, handwritten records, water management, storage, and textiles are acknowledged. Their later page placement does not claim a first invention or inevitable centralized organization. |
| Saw, Timber, Bridge, Wagon | These depict later steel or iron-reinforced workshop branches, not the first woodwork, crossing, or wheeled carrier. Copper/bronze saws, wood prepared with axes and wedges, timber crossings, and fourth-millennium BCE wagons existed earlier. |

Stable element and recipe IDs, existing ingredient pairs, saved discoveries, formulas, experiment history, favorites, and earned reward markers are retained. Legacy IDs such as `compass`, `nation`, `quill`, and `plow` now identify the corrected concepts. Existing save reconciliation unlocks Bronze for a previously discovered Polity or Iron for a previously discovered Lens and supplies the normal ore grants without retroactive Insight. New games still use the original gates. Page and category completion percentages can change after reclassification.

## Atlas features

Society, Knowledge, and Transport join the original categories. Village and City are Society; Papyrus, Map, Writing, and Scribe are Knowledge; Wheel and Cart are Transport. Legacy saves containing reclassified Metal Tool, Glass, Papyrus, Map, or City unlock Bronze Age automatically.

The Guide includes active-era category completion, **Unstudied** markers for elements with unresolved outgoing uses, and a three-credit **Insight** wallet. Insights unlock after Origins or three distinct failures. Five new unique failures earn one credit, new discoveries reset the visible `0/5` stall meter, repeats do nothing, and a full wallet pauses at `4/5`. Unlocking an age and completing an age challenge each award one credit up to the cap.

Spending Insight reveals one lead without automatically performing it. Selection prioritizes unknown results from the active age, then cross-age formulas using active-age elements, then other unlocked discoveries. Every revealed but unperformed formula remains under **Open leads** regardless of the selected page.

Recorded formulas, eligible Open Leads, and Recent Experiments have prepare-only actions: they fill both slots without combining, spending Insight, or recording an attempt. Locked leads keep their result hidden and their prepare action disabled until the required age unlocks. The Guide retains the latest 50 complete experiments, including repeated pairs, with discovery, known-result, no-reaction, or locked outcomes. Empty-slot submissions and hint requests are excluded; locked outcomes do not reveal the result.

The searchable **Recorded Formulas** archive lists every performed formula whose result belongs to the active age, including alternate routes. Formula records remain available after their attempts leave Recent Experiments. Older saves with performed recipe IDs populate the archive without inventing experiment history; element-only saves do not imply that a formula was performed.

Three themed collections appear separately from category completion: **Stormwatch** (Mist, Cloud, Rain, Storm, Lightning), **Harvest Table** (Field, Crop, Flour, Dough, Bread), and **Ironworkers** (Iron Ore, Bellows, Bloomery, Iron, Iron Tool). Each first completion awards +1 Insight, capped at three. Completing a collection with a full wallet still consumes its one-time reward; overflow is not banked. Loading a save does not award collection rewards.

Agriculture branches from Field into Crop. Crop + Heat creates broad Food, while Bread has one authored route: Crop + Tool → Flour, Flour + Water → Dough, and Dough + Heat → Bread.

## Rainmaker

Rainmaker is an isolated replay challenge targeting Rain, starting with only Fire, Water, Earth, and Air and allowing only recipes with Origins results, including the expanded Origins content. The existing four-attempt winning route is unchanged; the run still ends when Rain is discovered. Each complete combination counts as an attempt, including repeats and failures; empty submissions do not count. It has no campaign age unlocks or hints.

Challenge discoveries, formulas, experiments, and occupied slots do not change the campaign. A completed run changes campaign metadata only through explicit **Save result**: the first saved completion awards +1 Insight up to the three-credit cap and records the best attempt count. The reward is consumed even at a full wallet; later saved wins can improve the best score but never award another Insight. Returning, abandoning, or retrying does not copy challenge discoveries into the Atlas. Runs are transient: reloading abandons the run and returns to the campaign, with no active-run resume.

Save result waits for confirmed storage before acknowledging completion or granting its reward. Saving, Return, and Retry are disabled while confirmation is pending; a failed write leaves records and rewards untouched and permits retry unless save protection is latched.

## Content

Game definitions live in `src/game/content`. Every recipe is an unordered pair with one deterministic result. Eras define unlock requirements, granted elements, and optional challenge landmarks. `validateContent` checks references, duplicate pairs, era contracts, and simulates gated recipe reachability before applying each era grant.

Content validation simulates actual play: resolve recipes only in unlocked eras, check landmark requirements, grant newly unlocked elements, and repeat. Circular era gates and unreachable page content fail validation before build.

## Historical research database (Phase 2)

Authoring research lives in [`src/game/content/research.json`](src/game/content/research.json), validated by [`research.ts`](src/game/content/research.ts) and [`research.test.ts`](src/game/content/research.test.ts). This is version-controlled JSON, not a hosted database. It is intentionally separate from the gameplay content exports: it adds no player-facing UI, save fields, age gates, recipes or runtime research payload.

The initial batch covers **10 of 144 elements**: Wayfinding (legacy Compass), Lens, Polity (legacy Nation), Metal, Writing Reed (legacy Quill), Writing, Road, Market, Ard (legacy Plow), and Glass. It contains **14 consulted sources and one pending source**. These are focused research records, not exhaustive histories or certification of every claim in an existing description. The remaining 134 elements have no research record yet.

| Record field | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `elementId`, `elementName` | Stable gameplay ID and current display name; stale names and unknown IDs fail validation. |
| `historicalOverview` | A sourced overview, with references to the central source registry. |
| `approximateDates` | Regional dates labeled as dated evidence, earliest known evidence, regional development, widespread adoption, or undated; each has a precision and explanatory note. |
| `associatedRegions` | Relevant findspots or regions, not necessarily invention locations. |
| `archaeologicalEvidence` | Artifact, excavated-context, textual-evidence or interpretation entries, distinguished as attested, hypothetical or disputed. This can be empty when no material evidence is established. |
| `historicalSignificance` | A sourced explanation of why the evidence matters. |
| `independentDevelopments` | Separately referenced regional developments when supported. Writing records Mesopotamian, Egyptian, Chinese and Mesoamerican primary inventions; missing dates are not invented. An empty list means none is established by this source set, not that no independent development occurred. |
| `historicalUncertainty` | Dating, definition, preservation, provenance and interpretation limits, including any gaps in access or regional coverage. |
| `gameplaySimplifications` | What the page assignment, generic element, legacy ID or symbolic combinations omit; these are not historical prerequisites. |
| `furtherResearch` | Open questions and source leads, including inaccessible material that must not support accepted claims. |

Each source records its **title, authors (where credited), institution, source type, original URL, publication date when known, and verification receipt**. An unknown publication date is `null`; artifact dates and API metadata-update timestamps are not publication dates. A verified receipt records the check date, actual accessed URL, access level and supporting passage or catalog fields. Museum-owned APIs and legitimate article archives may supply original records when the main site is unavailable.

Verification is **claim- and passage-specific**, not blanket approval of an entire website or paper. The Met API entries establish catalog metadata, not unread descriptive essays. The Sion ploughing study was checked through its publisher-deposited abstract in Crossref; the full paper was not accessible. The British Museum scribal palette returned HTTP 403 and remains pending. Earlier reference links in this README do not automatically become verified research sources.

Prioritize excavation publications, peer-reviewed archaeology/archaeometry, museum collections and institutional research. World History Encyclopedia is supplementary regional context, not a replacement for primary evidence. AI-assisted searches may find leads, but **read the original supporting passage before accepting a claim or updating game content**. Check the title, credited author/institution, geography, dating method and alternative interpretations. Retain discrepancies and limitations rather than resolving them through an AI summary.

For new records, add sources and their receipts first, then reference verified source IDs from overviews, dates, evidence, significance and independent developments. Pending sources may appear only in `furtherResearch`. Validation rejects duplicate records/source IDs, dangling references, missing required fields, invalid web URLs/dates and historical dates without sources or geography. Year-resolved dates are reserved for specific dated evidence, such as the Sweet Track's construction, not exact invention/adoption claims. Unknown beginnings and fictional Origins remain undated; first surviving evidence is not widespread adoption.

Run the focused authoring checks with `npm test -- src\game\content\research.test.ts`, then the normal validation commands above. These checks enforce the research structure and citation relationships, **not historical truth or live URL availability**; original-source review remains necessary.

## Saves And Settings

Progress is stored locally in the browser under `unwritten-atlas-progress`. Version 7 includes discovered elements, performed formulas, Insight credits and stall progress, age challenge reward markers, Open Leads, failed unordered pairs, unlocked ages, and the active page, plus favorites, sound preference, the latest 50 experiments, collection reward markers, and Rainmaker completion/best-score records. In-flight challenge state is not saved. There are no accounts or cloud sync.

Campaign progress is saved automatically and restored on reload. Settings controls the persistent sound preference; favorites also persist immediately. Manual file import and export are not available. Existing local saves remain compatible. Storage failures are reported, so continued in-memory play is not a guarantee that progress was saved.

A missing save starts a writable campaign. An existing save that is invalid, unsupported, too large, or unreadable instead starts in-memory play with autosaving blocked for that page session. Nothing overwrites the original, including reset, preference changes, and challenge-result saving. The warning remains visible; reload after addressing the storage problem to try loading again. Reloading discards unsaved session changes.

Writes use browser Web Locks to serialize cooperating tabs and check the original stored snapshot inside the lock. Clean tabs automatically adopt valid external updates on storage events, focus, visibility changes, or before the next action, without replaying rewards. Sound updates immediately; still-valid occupied slots are retained. External reset or save removal restores fresh campaign defaults. If an external save changes while this tab has unsaved progress, saving and adoption pause until reload, preserving this tab's in-memory session rather than silently discarding or merging it. Invalid or unreadable external updates also pause saving. Ordinary quota failures remain retryable when the stored snapshot has not changed.

Safe autosaving requires Web Locks support (and a secure context, including localhost). Unsupported browsers allow in-memory play but do not fall back to unsafe writes. All open tabs should run the updated app: older versions and external scripts do not honor its lock, so they cannot be guaranteed to cooperate. This is local browser synchronization, not cloud sync or a backup.

Earlier saves migrate to v7, preserving old hint credits as Insights within the three-credit cap. Favorites, experiment history, and challenge records default to empty, and sound defaults to enabled. Already-completed collections in v1-v6 saves are marked rewarded without retroactive Insight; existing age challenge reward migration is retained. Migration removes failed pairs that are valid in the current graph, and successful formulas defensively clear stale failures. Correct guesses from locked pages remain free Open Leads. Historical discoveries reconcile matching age unlocks and grants without retroactive unlock rewards.

Reset returns the campaign to Origins with four starting elements and three Insights. It clears discoveries beyond the starters, formulas, leads, failure progress, favorites, experiment history, reward markers, and challenge completion/best-score records, while preserving the sound preference.
