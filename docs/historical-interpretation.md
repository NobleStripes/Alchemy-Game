# Chronology and historical interpretation

[Back to the project overview](../README.md)

The **Three-Age System** originated in the classification of Scandinavian archaeological collections by Christian Jurgensen Thomsen, published in 1836. It remains useful in particular European, Near Eastern, and other archaeological traditions, but is not a universal ladder of human development. Some regions have different sequences or no distinct Bronze Age. Stone, wood, bone, copper, bronze, and iron technologies also overlap rather than replace one another instantaneously.

| Page | Date label and scope |
| --- | --- |
| Origins | Undated, symbolic gameplay prologue. The four-root theory is documented Greek philosophy; life-making and puzzle transformations are fictional or simplified mechanics, not historical events. |
| Stone Age | c. 3.3 million years ago–c. 3300 BCE. The opening anchor is the Lomekwi stone-tool evidence in Kenya, not the appearance of Homo sapiens. The closing anchor is a conventional Near Eastern Bronze Age transition, not the end of stone-tool use everywhere. This intentionally broad page combines Paleolithic and later Neolithic lifeways; it is not one regional chronology. |
| Bronze Age | c. 3300–1200 BCE is a conventional approximate Near Eastern/eastern Mediterranean frame, not a date for the first copper or the arrival of bronze in every region. Local chronologies differ: the National Museum of Denmark uses 1700–500 BCE for its Bronze Age. Earlier copper-working/Chalcolithic traditions are not a separate playable page here. |
| Iron Age | From c. 1200 BCE is an approximate Near Eastern transition toward wider iron use, not the first iron object. There is no global end date: the Ashmolean uses c. 800 BCE–43 CE for Britain. The former c. 500 CE endpoint has been removed because it mixed regional conventions into a misleading universal range. |

**Earliest evidence is not widespread adoption.** A rare artifact, experimental process, or first surviving written reference does not establish when a technology became common. Nor does absence of surviving evidence prove absence of a practice. Page placement may represent an intensified craft tradition or an optional later refinement, not invention. Dates and political labels are geographically dependent; Classical Antiquity can overlap iron-using societies rather than follow a worldwide Iron Age boundary.

## Classical elements: historical theory, symbolic gameplay

The four-element theory is **documented historical natural philosophy**, not a fictional invention of the game. **Empedocles of Acragas**, in Greek Sicily, was a major early formulator of the four-root theory in the **fifth century BCE**: fire, water, earth and air were fundamental constituents of nature. The surviving poetic fragments and later accounts are textual evidence for the theory, not scientific proof of its validity. Precise biographical dates and some fragment interpretations remain uncertain.

**Aristotle** developed an account in the **fourth century BCE** linking elemental bodies to qualities: fire hot/dry, air hot/moist, water cold/moist, and earth cold/dry. His account distinguishes elementary bodies from ordinary examples; it should not be conflated with Empedocles' roots and forces or modern chemical composition. The air root discussed in Empedocles' scholarship is *aither*, upper atmospheric air, not simply modern breathing air.

| Aspect | Classification |
| --- | --- |
| Four-element theory | Documented Greek natural philosophy, superseded as chemistry |
| Empedocles' four roots | Fifth-century BCE Greek philosophical theory |
| Aristotle's elemental qualities | Fourth-century BCE Greek natural philosophy |
| Origins gameplay chronology | Undated, symbolic prologue; not a historical date for the theory |
| Element-combination recipes | Symbolic or simplified game mechanics, not reconstructions of either theory |
| Modern scientific status | Fire accompanies combustion, a chemical reaction; pure water is the compound H2O; earth denotes terrestrial matter, not one chemical element; atmospheric air is a mixture of gases |

This is a particular Greek intellectual tradition, **not a universal belief of every ancient civilization or every Greek thinker**. For comparison, Chinese *wuxing* discusses five phases named wood, fire, earth, metal and water through cycles of generation and overcoming. It belongs to a distinct cultural and intellectual context; similar labels do not establish identical theories, a common origin or independent inventions of the Greek roots.

The four starters retain their IDs, categories, icons, age, starting status and recipes. Each description keeps its opening flavor sentence separate from a labeled **Historical context** summary. The Atlas supplies the verified explanation, century-level philosophical dates, textual evidence, uncertainty and citations; Origins itself remains undated. No Classical Antiquity chapter or new progression requirement is introduced.

The earlier blanket subtitle, Atlas placement label and chronology-table phrase describing Origins' "essences" as fictional have been clarified. References below and the [research registry](../src/game/content/research.json) support these corrections. Fictional life-making, symbolic Metal creation and unrelated gameplay gates remain disclosed as such.

## Unlock requirements are fictional gameplay gates

All four gates were reviewed and retained, with their historical limits made explicit:

| Page | Current gate | Historical interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Origins | Four starting essences; no unlock requirements | A fictional combination sandbox, not a reconstruction of geological or biological chronology. |
| Stone Age | Life, Land, Tree, Rock, Animal; Human grant | Natural-world discoveries organize the prologue. This is not a claim that humans appeared only after every listed discovery, or that all toolmakers were Homo sapiens. |
| Bronze Age | Village, Pottery, Metal, Quarry; Copper Ore grant | A settlement-and-materials gameplay milestone. Neither pottery nor a formal stone quarry is a universal prerequisite for metallurgy, and native copper and copper smelting predate widespread bronze. The grant does not date the discovery of copper ore. |
| Iron Age | Bronze, Forge, Law, City; Iron Ore grant | A workshop-and-civic gameplay milestone. Ironworking does not universally require a preceding Bronze Age, written law, or cities. These are not claims about how every society adopted iron. |

Landmarks, grants, discovery goals (18 / 16 / 23 / 6), and rewards are unchanged. Recipe inputs likewise express game associations, not necessary historical prerequisites or workable manufacturing instructions. Alternate routes do not mean societies had to follow the same path.

## Element review

The earlier review covered **144 element descriptions**, including the 24 additions beyond the 120-element catalog. Three sourced Iron Age additions now bring the catalog to **147**; the requested 115-element count is no longer current. Generic natural phenomena and plausible nonexclusive craft descriptions are retained rather than assigned unsupported invention dates. The material corrections are:

| Element or group | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Compass | Reworked as **Wayfinding**, remaining in Stone Age. Magnetic maritime navigation belongs much later (documented in medieval China and introduced in Europe in the late twelfth century); simply moving a mariner's compass into Iron Age would not solve that error. Existing magnetic ingredient pairs are explicitly symbolic, and an additional nonmagnetic route is available. |
| Lens | Moved from Stone to Iron Age as an optional optical concept, not a dated ancient optical craft, spectacles or a telescope. The Neo-Assyrian object often called the Nimrud Lens is probably a crystal inlay; its optical use is disputed and is not proof of widespread ancient lens technology. Earlier map and voyage routes remain available without Lens. |
| Nation | Reworked as **Polity** and moved to Bronze Age, reflecting city-states and kingdoms rather than modern nation-states. Neither cities, written laws, nor mapped borders are universal prerequisites for political organization. |
| Metal, Copper, Metal Tool, Tin | Origins Metal is explicitly symbolic, not smelting before humanity. Native copper-working and later copper smelting precede widespread bronze use. Tin-bearing ore must be smelted; washing alone does not produce tin metal. |
| Quill | Reworked as **Writing Reed**, a broad plant-based writing category, not a feather quill. The consulted pen catalog gives a broad Egyptian period, not the first stylus, brush or cut-nib pen or this specimen's nib construction. A plant-based route is added; the old animal/scribe association remains an explicitly symbolic compatibility route. |
| Writing, Papyrus, Law | Writing includes Mesopotamian proto-cuneiform and Egyptian hieroglyphic traditions; early accounting signs are not automatically evidence of recorded speech. A clay-and-reed route avoids making papyrus or ink necessary for all writing without identifying an excavated tablet's actual tool. Papyrus is made from strips of pith, not generic pulped fibres. Law can be customary and oral, not necessarily written. |
| Road, Market, Trade | Retained on their gameplay pages with earlier timber trackways and exchange networks acknowledged. Exchange need not involve coins, carts, or ironworking; imported goods and redistribution records alone do not establish a formal marketplace. Its earliest local evidence remains an open research question here. |
| Plow | Reworked as **Ard**, a simple scratch plow often made of wood. Earlier digging sticks, hoes, and ards do not require bronze. It makes shallow furrows, not the soil-turning action of a later moldboard plow. |
| Glass | Bronze Age placement is retained for early beads and vessels, often colored or opaque. Silica needs flux and controlled heat. Later glassblowing (first century BCE), clear optical glass, window glass, and spectacles are not implied by the early material. |
| Brick, Lodestone, Human, Primordial Soup, Dough, Cave Painting | Brick becomes **Mud Brick**, distinct from widespread fired brick. Magnet becomes naturally occurring **Lodestone**, not manufactured prehistoric magnets. Human represents hominin makers; life's origins and cave art meanings are not presented as settled facts. Early dough need not be leavened. |
| Boat, Anchor, Bellows, Iron, Bloomery, Sickle, Steel | Earlier watercraft, stone anchors, bellows, and non-iron sickles are acknowledged. Rare and meteoritic iron precedes wider ironworking. Bloomery reduction produces a solid bloom, not cast liquid iron; steel properties depend on composition and heat treatment. |
| Empire, Manuscript, Irrigation, Granary, Loom, Textile | Earlier empires, handwritten records, water management, storage, and textiles are acknowledged. Their later page placement does not claim a first invention or inevitable centralized organization. |
| Saw, Timber, Bridge, Wagon | These depict later steel or iron-reinforced workshop branches, not the first woodwork, crossing, or wheeled carrier. Copper/bronze saws, wood prepared with axes and wedges, timber crossings, and fourth-millennium BCE wagons existed earlier. |
| Rotary Quern, Lathe, Alphabet | Optional page assignments, not universal invention dates. British rotary-grinding evidence is distinguished from older grinding; proposed Egyptian lathe pivots remain hypothetical and abstract-only; alphabetic precedents predate the Iron Age frame, and literacy need not use an alphabet. See the sourced records and access limits in the [research guide](research.md). |

Stable element and recipe IDs, existing ingredient pairs, saved discoveries, formulas, experiment history, favorites, and earned reward markers are retained. Legacy IDs such as `compass`, `nation`, `quill`, and `plow` now identify the corrected concepts. Existing save reconciliation unlocks Bronze for a previously discovered Polity or Iron for a previously discovered Lens and supplies the normal ore grants without retroactive Insight. New games still use the original gates. Page and category completion percentages can change after reclassification.
