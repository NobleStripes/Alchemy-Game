# Optional content

[Back to the project overview](../README.md) | [Gameplay and Atlas](gameplay.md) | [Catalog plan](catalog-plan.md)

Optional discoveries, collection objectives and isolated learning labs extend the main campaign without increasing existing age-completion requirements. Core additions reuse version 7 saves. DLC is deferred; existing archives retain inactive records for backup. Reading history is always free and never required for rewards.

Open **Challenges** in the persistent main navigation for Rainmaker and all eleven Learning Labs, their completion status and best saved attempts. The isolated runner stays mounted when visiting Play or Atlas; return to Challenges to resume. Leaving a run restores focus to its originating card. The former Guide lab listing and separate Rainmaker header shortcut are removed; mobile Play still has only Elements, Combine and Guide tabs. Runs are transient across reloads, while existing v7 records and first-completion rewards are unchanged.

This guide describes implemented content. The [catalog plan](catalog-plan.md) separately tracks proposed discoveries, regional modules and research-only work; evidence-blocked candidates are not playable additions.

The playable catalog is base-only: **225 elements and 291 recipes** across five ages. Existing optional base discoveries and regional histories remain included; they are not DLC. There is no Regional Modules manager or playable pilot. See [legacy archive compatibility](saves.md#regional-modules-and-durable-archives).

## Classical Antiquity and Iron Age branches

Classical Antiquity is a mandatory core gameplay page with 45 core discoveries,
not a removable regional pack. It unlocks after Iron, Iron Tool, Road and Market.
Its approximate 800 BCE-500 CE gameplay frame covers overlapping Mediterranean
histories, not a universal period or a necessary stage in every society's history.
Earlier precedents keep their own dates, and regional comparisons do not imply
simultaneous Classical ages. Its expansion branches are optional for completing
the unchanged core age objectives.

All 38 discoveries added after the initial seven Age V discoveries are optional even for the unchanged Age V completion target. Existing Scroll, Coin and Aqueduct retain their earlier ages and recipes. The [Age V branch table](gameplay.md#optional-age-v-branches) includes inquiry, civic life, architecture, commerce, scholarship and intellectual practices, with recipe spoilers behind a disclosure.

Optional Iron Age Rotary Quern, Lathe and Alphabet branches provide additional grinding, woodworking and writing discoveries. Their histories distinguish earlier precedents from regional evidence rather than dating all three inventions to that gameplay page. See the [five gameplay pages](gameplay.md#five-gameplay-pages) for progression requirements.

## Production foundations

The expansion toward 250 begins with six production foundations: **Grinding Stone, Spindle, Yarn, Kiln, Fired Brick and Tin Ore**, plus eleven optional formulas. Full histories cover every addition and six existing backlog elements: Flour, Fibre, Loom, Textile, Tin and Mud Brick.

These discoveries expose useful intermediate stages without replacing legacy formulas. Spindle-free Yarn remains available in Stone Age gameplay; firing a suitable clay unit differs from drying Mud Brick; and recognizing tin ore differs from reducing it to metal. The [foundation chain table](catalog-plan.md#implemented-recipes-and-intermediate-chains) contains recipe spoilers and the historical limits of every new route.

The catalog plan classifies regional and research-only proposals separately and pairs later element milestones with research milestones. Unverified candidates remain blocked. Legacy routes, gates, rewards and v7 saves are retained. Source receipts disclose summary, preprint, selected-passage and abstract-only limits; automated checks are not historical peer review.

## Stone Age materials and watercraft

The third batch added **Resin, Adhesive, Dugout Canoe and Paddle**, all optional Stone Age discoveries, plus seven formulas. That milestone reached **219 elements and 277 recipes**. Eight full histories cover the four additions and four existing gaps: Stone Tool, Cordage, Net and Hearth. Its Atlas snapshot was **135 histories**, leaving **84 gaps**.

Resin is a natural conifer resource, not plant gum, birch tar or every tree's sap. Its preparation represents one adhesive tradition rather than universal glue chemistry. Dugouts are distinct from the existing later Boat page, and paddles are human-powered equipment, not sails. The [audit and evidence limits](catalog-plan.md#milestone-219-stone-age-materials-and-watercraft) distinguish regional objects, contextual dates and interpretations. Paddle has no unsupported numerical date.

All four discoveries are reachable without later Timber or metal tools. Every new node has an outgoing use; the Paddle/Boat journey remains gated to Bronze Age Voyage. No existing formula, age goal, collection, challenge, reward or save field changes. Reading remains optional and free.

## Specialized production and water power

The fourth batch adds **Pottery Wheel, Crucible, Annealing and Plumb Line** in Bronze Age and **Waterwheel and Watermill** in Classical Antiquity, with eleven formulas. Current totals are **225 elements, 291 recipes and 147 full histories**, leaving **78 original gaps**. Every new node has an onward use. Pottery, Wheel, Mold, Quarry, Crop and Bread also receive full histories.

The [acceptance audit](catalog-plan.md#milestone-225-specialized-production-measurement-and-water-power) separates historical development, regional evidence and gameplay placement. Pottery remains possible without a wheel; an arbitrary pot is not a crucible; annealing is not smelting, alloying or steel hardening; a plumb line is not a complete survey kit; and powered milling does not begin the history of grinding. Abstract-only and reconstructed evidence is labeled.

All six discoveries are optional. The Bronze Age additions are reachable without later tools; water-power results remain locked until Age V. Neither water-power discovery changes Age V unlocks or completion, and the entire older catalog alone still supports every age challenge. Saves remain version 7, and reading remains discovered-only, explicit and free.

## Craft, records and engineering

The second batch adds **15 discoveries and 27 recipes**, bringing the catalog to **215 elements and 270 recipes**:

- **Stone Age:** Axe, Adze, Wedge, Fishhook and Fishing Weir.
- **Bronze Age:** Smelting Furnace, Clay Tablet, Sealed Tablet and Standardized Weights.
- **Iron Age:** Iron Chisel, Gouge and Wax Tablet.
- **Classical Antiquity:** Pulley, Water Screw and Vault.

All additions have full histories and every route has a cited review. Fifteen original research gaps also receive entries, bringing Atlas coverage to **127 histories**, with **88 gaps** remaining. These discoveries change no age gates, grants, landmarks, goals, collections, challenge definitions, rewards or save fields.

The [acceptance audit](catalog-plan.md#milestone-215-craft-records-and-engineering) records semantic distinctions, chronology and evidence limits. A seal is an implement, its impression is a mark, and an envelope is a separate covering: Sealed Tablet groups documented arrangements without automatically creating an envelope. Standardized Weights describes regional conventions, not one ancient worldwide unit. Wax boards have earlier precedents; the Iron Age page is not their invention date.

Timber retains its old Iron Age assignment even though splitting and worked wood are much older. Specialized tools have symbolic downstream associations, not proven uses of the cited broken chisel or gouge. Vitruvius supplies textual evidence for machines, not excavated first examples or sole-inventor proof.

## Landmark track and learning labs

The Guide has an **optional landmark track** for Agora (civic), Library (knowledge), Aqueduct (engineering), and Mint (economic), with supporting-discovery requirements and one-time collection rewards.

Four additional isolated challenges—**Civic Foundations**, **Organization of Knowledge**, **Engineering the City**, and **Economic Networks**—bring the learning-lab total to eleven, alongside unchanged Rainmaker. They reuse existing recipes and sourced histories, preserve all earlier collection and challenge IDs, and add no core age requirements or save-version changes. Aqueduct retains its Iron Age assignment; its optional Age V objective also asks for Masonry and Arch.

Challenge discoveries do not become campaign discoveries. Results and rewards are recorded only through explicit **Save result**. See [optional Age V objectives](gameplay.md#optional-age-v-objectives) for supporting discoveries, collections and labs, and [Rainmaker](gameplay.md#rainmaker) for isolated-run and reward rules.

## Engineering

The optional engineering branch adds **Slaked Lime, Lime Mortar, Volcanic Ash, Concrete, Road Network and Public Bath**, reusing Arch, Aqueduct and Amphitheater. Its sourced histories distinguish lime hydration and carbonation, Roman pozzolanic concrete from modern Portland cement, and volcanic ash from wood ash.

Two-input formulas omit real production stages; the ash recipe symbolizes encountering geological material, not making it by heating a rock. Aqueduct history covers gravity, surveyed gradients, maintenance and unequal water distribution, alongside pre-Roman precedents.

See [engineering materials and infrastructure](gameplay.md#optional-engineering-materials-and-infrastructure) for recipes, gameplay ages and process cautions.
